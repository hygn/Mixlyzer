# AGENTS: audioengine/native/ (Mixlyzer)

The details of the native audio engine (Rust): its process, devices and outputs, the time stretch, the deck, the mixer, MIDI. The root `AGENTS.md`'s rules (run, layers, owners, change guidelines) apply here too; read it first.

## The engine
- `native/`: the default engine, Rust (`Cargo.toml`, `src/`), a process of its own so playback never waits for the GUI's GIL, garbage collector or busy frames (measured: no dropout at a 20 ms buffer with the time stretch, 14 busy processes or the GUI thread holding the GIL). `client.py` is the GUI's side: it makes the control block (shared memory; the layout is `src/ipc.rs`'s and `client.py`'s, keep them together: a sequence-locked state area the engine writes, a command ring the GUI writes, records checked by CRC-32) and starts `bin/mixlyzer-audio --shm NAME --size BYTES --parent PID` (it exits when the GUI process is gone); its console lines (`src/log.rs`, `log_line!`: "[AudioEngine] ...", queued to a thread of their own, so a pipe's busy reader never holds up the engine thread or an output callback) come through a pipe into the app's console, log and startup splash (`client._relay_console`): at the start its version, VOLK, each device opened (API, mode: shared / exclusive event driven / polled, sample format, rate, channels, period, buffer, stream latency, output thread), the threads' scheduling, the MIDI input. `NativeFeeder` has `PCMFeeder`'s methods, `NativeClicks` / `NativeTones` the click and tone mixers' (the metronome drives them); a dead engine is started again with the track and the settings it had, and Settings > Audio > Engine's Restart Audio Engine (the shortcut `playback.restart_engine`, Alt+Shift+E) restarts it by hand, the last resort when an output or the engine stays stuck (`NativeFeeder.restart_engine`: the old one stopped, ended if it does not answer; the track, its place and its play carried over). In the engine (`src/`): `device.rs` the output reading a ring the engine thread fills to the output buffer (a flush fades out over 10 ms in the callback, a raised cosine as every start / stop / seek fade (`dsp::fade_gain`): a linear 4 ms ramp clicked on low notes; before a flush the ring is topped up to two fades past the frame being read, so the fade-out runs whole; stopped off unity with the time stretch, the stream from where play would start is prepared a little each tick (`Standby::Start`), so CUE / PLAY never restart a stream in one tick; device lists are made on threads of their own), through the audio API of Settings > Audio (`native_audio_api`, changed while playing): WASAPI (Windows, `wasapi.rs`: IAudioClient3's low-latency shared stream at the mix format (raw where the device allows: `AUDCLNT_STREAMOPTIONS_RAW`, the system's enhancements such as Loudness Equalization left out, else opened again with them; its session opted out of ducking, so another app's call does not lower it; the system volume and the app's mixer slider are the user's and stay), or with `native_exclusive` WASAPI exclusive in float32 / int32 / 24 bits (in 32, packed) / int16, JUCE's order (the DDJ-400: packed 24), both at the device's smallest period but 256 frames at least (`PERIOD_FRAMES_MIN`, as rekordbox asks the DDJ-400 for: the USB Audio class driver's GetBuffer waits ~0.8 ms each period and now and then 10 ms, which outlasts a 3 ms period, runs the device dry and leaves its events every ~8 ms; measured on the DDJ-400: 132 frames 5 restarts in 80 s, 192 one, 256 none); an exclusive stream is polled: kept two periods full (`POLL_FILL_PERIODS`: the event-driven one's latency; the driver may give a polled stream far more, the DDJ-400 2048 frames, 46 ms, which filled whole was all latency) and topped up every 1 ms, event driven only where the device refuses that (an event-driven exclusive stream whose output thread wakes once a period late runs the device dry, and the DDJ-400's USB Audio class driver then falls back to a 10 ms tick taking one buffer per tick, for good: events 10.00 ms apart at a 5.8 ms period, GetBuffer 0.04 -> 0.75 ms, the device's own play position (IAudioClock) at 58 % of its rate, the headphones a second behind; only Stop / Reset / Start brings it back; measured with the thread held 15, 30 and 60 ms: event driven at 256 frames, rekordbox's way (JUCE's exclusive stream, its buffer one period), fell into the tick at the first hold every time, polled kept 2 periods (11.6 ms), 3 or the whole buffer it stayed at its rate through all three; rekordbox has a polled mode too, off by default: `WasapiPolling`); every stream (the main device's and the others', exclusive or shared) is watched while it runs and started again in place (its bridges refilled) when it is not keeping pace: an exclusive stream's last 8 events 1.8 periods apart on average, the frames it took over a second below 95 % of its rate (events slower than the period without a long gap: the DDJ-400 seen at a 10 ms period taking 64 %, its headphones a second behind), or no event for 200 ms (a second in which the output thread itself woke late is left out: its lateness, not the device's pace); more than 3 times a minute it fails and is opened again by its owner, an exclusive one at the next period of a ladder (256 frames, 512, the device's default, then 256 again: never kept at a period that does not keep pace; `cargo build --features fault` with `MIXLYZER_AUDIO_FAULT=<device>:<from s>:<to s>` makes a device fall behind, tests only); a bridge that keeps skipping a surplus (more than twice in 30 s, any backend) has its device opened again by the outputs' thread; a bridge's reader held up more than three of its callbacks (20 ms at least) starts again as at a restart (filled to its buffer, its loop settled anew: followed on, the catch-up was taken for drift, 1000 ppm for tens of seconds); the `fault` build also records a device's events (`MIXLYZER_AUDIO_PROBE=<device>`: wake-ups, driver call times and its IAudioClock position to %TEMP%/mixlyzer-probe-*.csv), holds its thread once at given times (`MIXLYZER_AUDIO_STALL=<device>:<s>:<ms>,...`), leaves a stream not keeping pace as it is (`MIXLYZER_AUDIO_NO_RECOVER=1`) and picks its polling (`MIXLYZER_AUDIO_POLL=<device>:<periods>`, 0 event driven; `MIXLYZER_AUDIO_POLL_FILL=<device>:<periods>`, the level kept, 0 the whole buffer; `MIXLYZER_AUDIO_FORMAT=int24/24,...` only those formats tried; `MIXLYZER_AUDIO_QUERY=<device>` an event-driven stream's padding asked for every 1 ms between events). Why the polled stream survives where the event-driven one does not, seen on the probe: a late wake-up lets the device play one buffer that is never written back (event driven writes one buffer per event), so the driver is left with one buffer in flight and falls into its tick; polled, the next wake-up writes what is missing at once. The padding queries alone (no earlier writes) did not save the event-driven stream; rekordbox's own DDJ stream is fed by a thread that never sleeps, querying the driver in a loop (sampled: its syscalls the same as our polled thread's), so it refills at once too; at `native_exclusive_rate` when set: the session's rate, so it is applied at the next track load, `NativeFeeder.apply_session_format` starting the engine again after the old one let go of the device), WASAPI (cpal) / ALSA / CoreAudio (cpal's output; ALSA and CoreAudio with a ~5 ms device buffer) or JACK (Linux: cpal's JACK host); one that does not open falls back to cpal's (WASAPI's usual stream: ~32 ms), kept as the master's until the output is set again or the engine restarted (intended, as above); the engine thread opens and lets go of the master's device itself (intended: the master is silent meanwhile anyway). Nothing plays below 44.1 kHz (`ipc::MIN_RATE`, `controls.MIN_RATE`; the time stretch's band split needs it): a main device whose session rate is lower stops the engine at its start (the Python engine then refuses it too, so nothing plays), a headphones' or booth's device is not opened (`device::check_rate`), and the offline renders refuse a lower `--rate`. The buffer kept is the setting (2 ms and up; the engine thread ticks every 1 ms below 10 ms) plus what the device takes at once (`Ring.max_frames`), and the output callback wakes the engine thread after each read (`Ring.engine_thread`), so the ring is topped up before the next period. Commands carry the GUI's send time (protocol 2), and the engine measures play / pause / seek to the moment their sound is heard by the device's clock (the meter's "Response measured"; the driver's reported latency included, not the DAC's), The buffer monitor's histogram has 0.1 ms buckets up to twice the capacity (wider for big buffers), the lowest level over 10 s is the engine's own, and the dialog shows them in µs with a histogram (`ui/widgets/buffer_histogram.py`; the Python engine's in ms, 1 ms buckets). At a small buffer the time stretch stays fed: each tick fills the ring before the standby stretcher's work, which takes at most 30 % of the buffer's time a tick (one step at least), and a scrub let go resumes the tempo 80 ms ahead, its stream prepared meanwhile, at that very frame (no restart in one tick), `engine.rs` PCMFeeder's port (the timeline, the standby stretcher, jumps, scratch, the buffer monitor), `stretch.rs` the time stretch (its FFTs on realfft / rustfft, its vector work on VOLK through volk-rs: `vk.rs`), (`Stretcher`, 1.4 - 2.4 % of real time at 48 kHz rendered offline (5 tracks, -70 % to +25 %; 4.7 - 5.5 % before the band diet), the more the faster (the event detector runs on the source); grown from the Python one, whose event detector, RTPGHI with identity phase locking, time map and handovers it keeps; its parameters `stretch::Config`; `wrap`'s rounding without a library call, `round_even`: x86-64 has no rounding instruction before SSE4.1, and `rint` per bin was 6 - 8 % of the work): two phase vocoders on the same frames and time map, split in the spectrum about 1.5 kHz (`Config::mid_hz`, the crossover 100 Hz wide: each bin one vocoder's; a band split's transition, both bands carrying a tone with unrelated phases, beat), the crossover moved each frame to the low band's spectral valley nearest it, by 50 Hz a frame at most, within 300 Hz of 1.5 kHz (`valley`; only the bins its ramps cover set again): a voice's harmonic gliding across a fixed crossover was the sum of two vocoders' unrelated phases (a vibrato's harmonic at 1.5 kHz beat at -50 %, its level's 4 - 10 Hz movement +1 dB, now -6 dB); the low band's on the source decimated by `split.rs` (the most that keeps 1.65 kHz in 0.6 of its Nyquist frequency: 8 at 44.1 / 48 kHz; a Kaiser lowpass -90 dB from its Nyquist frequency; computed where it is read, a few windows cached: no memory per track; its RTPGHI's heap a bucket queue of 1/8 octave, `rtpghi_frame`), an 80 ms window (a voice's resolved harmonics, the bass; the crossover and the window chosen by listening among 1 kHz / 110 ms, 1.5 kHz / 110 or 80 ms and 2 kHz / 80 ms: a vibrato's partials move with their frequency and smear with the window's length, so the higher crossover takes the shorter window), synthesised at its own rate and brought to the full rate by the same lowpass, polyphase, where no later frame reaches (`upsample_low`); the upper band's (`UpperVocoder`) on the source itself, a 43 ms window (4 hops) every frame, not zero-padded (its bins a third fewer, a fifth of the work less; chosen by listening): above a voice's resolved harmonics the ear hears the pitch in the period's shape, and a vibrato or a glide moves its partials by tens of Hz within a long window, the bins' phases then at odds (the level beating at twice the vibrato's rate: the "LFO" of a slowed voice, at -70 %); its noise's bins turned at random each frame, by up to a turn per octave slowed (`NoiseBins`: a whole one from half speed down, none at unity or faster; the slower, the longer a bin's phase advance holds a noise still as a sinusoid, a metallic ring), their level kept (1 / sqrt(rho^2 + (1 - rho^2) 0.26), rho = sinc(the turn), measured on pink noise); a partial's bins kept (within 45 Hz of a peak 18 dB over the background of the bins within 180 Hz in the band's own spectrum: a voice's harmonics, a vibrato's smeared ones too; or within 50 Hz of a peak 8 dB over it in the event detector's 93 ms spectrum, from 1.1 kHz, below the crossover's range: a chord's partials, too close for the 43 ms window, resolved there; the background a noise's median, the bins' geometric mean over 0.81, `mark_partials`): with the band's spectrum alone a chord's partials were taken for noise and turned, its notes blurred slowed down (the harmonics' contrast over the floor between them 3.6 dB where the source's is 9.9; now 9.9 - 10.0); from half speed down only the partials' phases integrated (`rtpghi_frame`'s `only`: the noise's turned a whole turn are random whatever RTPGHI gives them). RTPGHI integrates the bins over -60 dB of the frame's loudest (`Config::rtpghi_tolerance`). A third vocoder above 6.5 kHz (a 128 ms window every third frame) kept the air a little noisier (its spectral flatness 0.95 of the source's against 0.8, slowed down) at about a quarter more of the work; the high band's WSOLA before it repeated the source's noise every few ms, heard as a pitch: the metallic ring of a slowed stretch. No window shortened about the events (the Python one's SELEBI moved held notes 1 - 2 dB at every hit). Each event's attack by the spectrum (`restore_attack`; its onset found in the source, `onset`, and carried to its destination on the time map; STFT frames of 1024 at 48 kHz, a quarter overlap): from the attack on, the source's energy the output lacks added bin by bin (sqrt(1 - |Y|^2 / |X|^2) of the source's frame about its attack, at its own speed), so a held note's bins, as loud in both, keep their stretched phase (the former crossfade to the source's waveform cancelled them at every hit: a tremolo at the beat's rate) and the hit is the source's, on the time map; before it, over the vocoders' smear, each bin louder than the source's where that music is (the time map) brought down to it, both frames cut 1 ms before the attack, the cut kept before it in time (a gate over the whole band ducked the held notes with the smear); the powers compared averaged over 150 Hz (a noise's single bins are as random as its phase: compared one by one, a room's or a reverb's tail was cut before each hit); nothing below 200 Hz (the bass keeps its phase), below 6.5 kHz (`Config::attack_whole_hz`) the added attack by the event's confidence of a dry or a kick attack (a sung syllable keeps its stretched voice), above it whole (over 9 kHz, what is left ahead of the attack is -26 to -35 dB under it (-31 dB the median) at -50 % and -33 %, 3 ms ahead, inside its backward masking: the upper band's short window gathers its smear there and the gate's 21 ms frames cut it a little less; the test allows -25 dB); `dsp.rs` varispeed / read_at / the platter (bit-identical to the Python ones; while scratching the engine sets the record's level by its speed, `SpeedGain`, as a needle's: silent held still, its level from 3 % of its speed on, so a jog touched while stopped or let go does not click on the sample held; a record let go near still while playing fades in), `mixers.rs` clicks and tones, `cursor.rs` the 1 kHz cursor poller, `clock.rs` the clock both processes share (Python's `perf_counter`). The engine thread runs every 2 ms (1 ms while scratching). Its threads' scheduling is `sched.rs`, from Settings > Audio's priority (`native_priority`, High by default) and power throttling (`native_power_throttling`, off by default), sent at once (`op::SET_PROCESS`, replayed at each start), what was got published (`st::THREADS` / `st::POWER`, shown under the meter): Windows the engine thread and its own WASAPI thread MMCSS Pro Audio and the 1 ms timer always, the setting the process's priority class and EcoQoS / timer coarsening opted out; Linux per thread (the engine's and the ALSA output's, its thread id written by the callback; a thread already real-time, JACK's, is left alone): High SCHED_FIFO, else rtkit's MakeThreadRealtime (zbus; RLIMIT_RTTIME set to rtkit's limit, SIGXCPU drops the thread to SCHED_OTHER), else nice -10 (Above normal -5; else rtkit's MakeThreadHighPriority), power throttling off a 1 ns timer slack (each thread sets its own) and uclamp's minimum at the top; macOS the engine thread High a time-constraint thread, Above normal QoS user-interactive, Normal QoS default (CoreAudio's thread is its own), power throttling off a latency-critical NSProcessInfo activity (objc2). The native engine is developed and measured on Windows; on Linux and macOS it is experimental (cross-checked with `cargo check --target`, not run there yet), and the dialog and the READMEs say so. The WASAPI thread counts its late wake-ups (a gap over 1.8 periods: shown under the meter), and the engine writes the last ticks before each gap (an underrun or a late output wake-up) to `%TEMP%/mixlyzer-audio-trace.log` (2 MB at most). Its buffer setting is this engine's own buffer, ahead of the device's latency (shown beside the meter; measured here: exclusive 6 ms, low-latency shared 12 ms, cpal's shared 32 ms), and the meter's dropouts are the engine's underruns. While scrubbing the time stretch plays the original at unity (a stream restart, ~10 ms of work, per drag position would not keep up), released it takes the tempo at once; seeks queued in one tick are made once (the last); a seek or a jump while playing off unity goes through the standby stretcher, prepared 1.5 ms of work a tick. The master's spectrum (`src/spectrum.rs`, protocol 8): while the GUI asks (`op::SET_SPECTRUM`: Settings > View's waveform bands in sight; replayed to a restarted engine), the main callback taps the master after its level (mono, a lock-free ring of its newest samples; nothing done while off) and the engine thread makes its power spectral density 30 times a second (a Hann-windowed FFT of the last ~85 ms, 4096 points at 48 kHz; one-sided, per Hz), averaged into 240 log-spaced bins from 20 Hz to 20.48 kHz (24 an octave; a bin narrower than the FFT's takes the FFT bin at its centre), sent as dB in u8 steps of 0.625 from -160 dB into a ring of 16 frames (`ipc.rs` SPECTRUM_*, each frame's seq written last; `NativeEngine.spectrum_frames`, one reader: the GUI thread; the bins' edges `audioengine.controls.spectrum_edges`); `cargo test spectrum::` checks a sine's bin, the bins summing to its power, the frame rate and silence. `--stretch-file` / `--scratch-file` render offline (tests/test_native_engine.py checks the stretch's level and attacks, every band's on the time map, nothing of them ahead at -50 % and -33 %, a held note steady and a quiet texture kept under drums, a vibrato steady and a noise kept noise slowed down, and the scratch path against the Python engine's).

## Its deck, the mixer and MIDI
- Its deck (`src/deck.rs` `Slot`: its player, `Engine`: track, timeline, stretcher, channel strip; and its controls, `Deck`), one place for the GUI's requests and a MIDI controller's, combined in the engine thread: rekordbox's CUE (stopped off the cue point: sets it on the nearest beat or track edge; held on it: plays until let go, PLAY while held plays on; playing: back to it and stops; a CUE point's CALL moves it there), beat / bar steps, CALL previous / next, a JumpCUE's JUMP (armed on the next beats, the GUI's former logic), the tempo with its range (6 / 10 / 16 % or WIDE, 0 - 200 %; a range change keeps the tempo inside the new range), the volume fader (audioengine/volume.py's curve, ported; the channel's TRIM knob is the level before it), the jog wheel (its mode: VINYL, or CDJ where the top's touch is ignored and a turn only bends; `act::JOG_MODE` a press, `MIDI_OPTIONS`' second u32; the lamps' bit 2, `LAMP_VINYL`; touch: the platter at a 20 ms delay, both rotation CCs move the record while it is held; let go while the platter turns, it is followed on until no message for 50 ms, as rekordbox's backspin; scratch 33 1/3 rpm by `midi_jog_ticks` a turn; bend: a turn a second +10 %, eased out over 80 ms; search (SHIFT): a bar on or back per eighth of a turn, the bar steps' rule, steps in a row each from the last one while it is not heard yet), the lamps (PLAY lit playing else blinking; CUE lit playing / previewing / on the cue point else blinking; a JumpCUE's pads, JUMP and its SHIFT delete, lit while it is set and blinking while armed; each binding carries its lamp, `core.controls.midi.lamp`; sent to the controller's output of the same name, only those that changed), the level meter (`midi.level_meter`, an output CC: the GUI peak meter's reading, the heard peak times `SET_METER_GAIN`, the GUI meter's segments lit (-42 - 0 dBFS) as their share of 0 - 127 falling 24 dB/s, at most every 20 ms; the rekordbox import takes it from ChannelLevel's output column). At the track's end the deck plays on (silence, on either engine): dragged or moved back, it plays again from there. `src/midi.rs` reads the controller with midir (WinMM, ALSA, CoreMIDI) on its own thread, which wakes the engine thread per message (its ports listed, opened and closed on threads of their own: tens of ms each, never the engine thread's; the port open or being opened asked for again is kept, and an open waits for the last close: WinMM refuses a port still being let go, MMSYSERR_ALLOCATED); `src/controller.rs` decodes them in the engine thread (relative encodings, 14-bit CCs, pitch bend, a direction's binding fired per step) to the deck, the mixer's controls to `MixParams`, and sends the lamps and the level meter; the messages bound to the GUI's actions (a JumpCUE pad's empty slot flagged as a SET), and all while Settings > MIDI learns, go to the GUI through a ring (`ipc.rs` MIDI_EVENTS; `NativeEngine.midi_events`). MIDI is the native engine's alone (the Python engine has none; External Sync closes the port; the toolbar's MIDI button, `ui/deck/midi_toggle.py`, switches the input on and off, `deckconfig.midi_enabled`, the port kept, lit as the engine reports the port: open, closed, not opened; its key `playback.midi_input`). The signal flow is player -> mixer strip -> mixer. The deck's player (`engine.rs` `Engine`) renders its track alone into its lane (the main device's `Ring`, with its flush fade and timing), a lane frame [music (ch), aids (ch)]: the listening aids (the metronome's clicks, ducking the music under them; reference tones) beside the music. The mixer (`src/mixer.rs`, a DJM-A9's channel and master section) runs in the main output's callback, so a fader, a knob or a CUE is heard after the device's latency alone, not the engine's buffer. The channel's strip (`Strip`; its settings `StripParams`, atomics the deck sets): trim -24 - +9 dB (the player's TRIM knob, kept in deck.json: `NativeFeeder.set_volume_trim`), Sound Color FX (`colorfx.rs`: SPACE, DUB ECHO, SWEEP, NOISE, CRUSH, FILTER, JET (a DJM-800's: a flanger's comb turned by hand, 6 to 0.12 ms, right in phase, left inverted, PARAMETER its feedback) or none, picked for the mixer, the channel's COLOR knob, the PARAMETER knob as the A9 manual's pages 38 - 41 give it), a 3-band EQ (`ipc::mix::EQ_MODE`: an isolator, Linkwitz-Riley 4th order at 246 Hz / 2484 Hz, a band from kill to +6 dB, or an EQ, shelves at 70 Hz / 13 kHz and a 1 kHz peak, -26 - +6 dB), its meter (pre-fader: the GUI's peak meter and a controller's level meter, held 50 ms by the engine) and its pre-fader CUE tap (the headphones), the channel fader (the deck's volume, three curves), the aids added after it all. The effects sit in the strip's three places of the A9's block diagram (manual page 93; `colorfx::Slot`): after the trim, after the EQ (before the CUE tap and the fader), after the fader; each effect in its own (`ColorType::slot`, `controls.COLOR_SLOTS`): NOISE before the EQ (the manual: the EQ shapes its noise), SPACE and DUB ECHO after the fader (their tail on with it down; DUB ECHO's time the deck's tempo), the others after the EQ. A stopped deck's strip runs on silence while an effect after its fader rings out. Then the master (its level, meter), the booth (its level, HI / LOW shelves -12 - +6 dB), the headphones (the CUE'd channel's pre-fader sound, and the master by the CUE / MASTER mix when it is mixed in: Settings > Audio > Headphones' Master in the headphones, `playbackconfig.headphones_master_mix` / `headphones_cue_mix`, `op::SET_HEADPHONES`; off for a controller that mixes the master into its headphones itself, as the DDJ-400 does in analog: then silent while no CUE is on; or MONO SPLIT: the CUE left, the master right; their level), each output's last stage of its own, keeping it at 0 dBFS at most so the system's mixer never limits it (`mixer::Clip`, `op::SET_CLIP`: the master's, the headphones', the booth's, each a hard clip, a tanh soft clip above -1.9 dBFS or a lookahead limiter, `dsp::Limiter`: the gain held at its least over a 1 ms window, released over 100 ms, averaged over the window, so no sample is over and nothing under 0 dBFS changes; the output 1 ms late, the master's delay added to the latency the deck's heard time counts, a bridge's not: it is in the samples; the engine's own default a hard clip, the app's setting sent at the start; the headphones and the booth take the master before the master's stage), each bus routed to a channel pair of the main device (`op::SET_ROUTING`: main, headphones, booth; the device opened with all its channels, the deck stereo: `st::OUT_CHANNELS`). The mixer's settings past the strip are keys (`ipc::mix`, `op::SET_MIXER` key and value, published at `MIX_OFFSET`: `controls.MIX_KEYS`, `NativeFeeder.set_mixer`; a restarted engine gets them as last reported, `NativeFeeder._replay`); a controller's DJM controls are engine acts (`act::COLOR_*`, `HEADPHONES_*`, `MASTER_LEVEL`, `BOOTH_*`, with their lamps) that `core.controls.midi` maps; the GUI's are the player's TRIM / EQ knobs and the toolbar's deck switches and levels (`ui/deck/deck_switches.py`: the EQ's ISO / EQ, the channel fader's curve, Sound Color FX). At its neutral settings (trim 0 dB, EQ flat, COLOR off, the fader at the top, the master at 0 dB) the mixer leaves the samples as they are, bit for bit. Its filters run on SIMD lanes (`simd.rs` `F4`: SSE2, NEON or scalar; `filters.rs` biquads and SVFs four lanes a pass, `fxkit.rs` delay lines, smoothers and a four-line FDN reverb a line a lane), the buses' sums and gains through VOLK. `--mix-file` renders a raw file through the mixer offline (any mixer setting, the blocks' time printed; `--stop-at S` the deck stopped then, its fader down). `tests/test_native_mixer.py` checks every stage on synthetic signals (JET's comb, the effects' places among them); the local `tests/native_mixer/verify_mix.py` / `verify_mix_real.py` on the user's tracks, `live_deck.py` the engine live on the user's devices. A lane that took nothing leaves the channel out: the deck plays as its player rendered it. The headphones and the booth may instead play on devices of their own (`op::SET_AUX_OUTPUT`, `src/outputs.rs` `AuxOutputs`: each its device, API, mode, rate, buffer and channel pair; Settings > Audio's Headphones / Booth pages, `playbackconfig.output_headphones` / `output_booth`: `config.outputconfig`, route "off" / "main_device" (channels of the main device) / "device"). One thread of theirs opens, keeps and lets go of the devices (an open takes tens of ms: the engine thread never waits for one); the buses naming the same device (and API and mode) share one stream of it (`bridge::Sink`: each bus read from its bridge into its channel pair, summed where pairs overlap; exclusive mode allows a device one stream: the headphones on a controller's 3-4 and the booth on its 1-2), at the device's mix format or the rate asked for (the first bus's: the headphones') (shared WASAPI at another rate falls back to cpal's stream, the system converting; the mode opened is published and the settings say when it is not the one asked for), again each second while it fails, run while the main device runs. The main callback, the clock the deck plays to, writes the bus into a bridge (`src/bridge.rs`), which the other device's callback reads at its own clock and rate: across the clock domains it resamples by the rates' ratio times the clocks' drift (a PI loop on the bridge's level as zita-ajbridge reads it: each side's device play position, stamped, and its mean queue (the writer's before its writes, the reader's after its reads: the level held is the bridge's lowest at any phase of the two periods), so neither the periods nor a shared stream's topping-up saw it; after 3 s settling the level the fill left is held, then moved to the buffer at 0.5 ms/s fed forward (a new buffer setting the same way), so the integral is the drift alone; a level more than 0.1 s over the buffer itself (the reader held up while the writer went on, also while it settles) is skipped at once, never held; the drift within 1000 ppm; a 64-tap Kaiser-windowed sinc interpolator, error under -85 dB to 20 kHz, to a lower rate cut at the output's Nyquist frequency: nothing repeated or dropped). The outputs run from their opening to their end (silence while nothing plays: no idle pause, so the bridges and their drift loops never restart). The engine's own WASAPI output fills its buffer (silence) before it starts (an exclusive stream's events may not come otherwise: the DDJ-400's USB driver sent one in 200 ms). Each open device has its `device::Port` (period, late wake-ups, failures, its output thread) and a backend reads a `device::Source` (the main ring, or a sink). Each bus's own output publishes its state and buffer monitor (`ipc::out`, `OUTPUTS_OFFSET`; `monitor.rs` `Monitor`, the engine's own too; `NativeEngine.aux_states`, `take_buffer_stats`' "outputs"), shown in its group with the clock drift (the loop's integral, ppm). `cargo test bridge::` checks the interpolator (the same rate, 48 to 44.1 kHz and back) and the drift following (jittery shared streams, the same rate and 44.1 kHz; a reader stalled 1.5 s while settling and later comes back to its buffer). Devices are listed per API with their channels (`op::LIST_DEVICES` with an API; `NativeEngine.devices(api)`). The deck's mixer channel is published with the deck's state (`st::CHANNEL_CUE`, `KNOBS`, `COLOR`), the mixer's settings and meters at `MIX_OFFSET` (`NativeEngine.mixer`). `cargo test` checks the deck's rules (`deck::`: the CUE / PLAY table, CALL, the beat steps, the jog's search, seeks a tick never overrunning the ring, play after CUE showing the cue point (the frames queued before a start are the deck standing there: `Timeline::hold_until`), JumpCUE, the tempo range and the lamps, on a track of the test's own, `shm::Pcm::owned`, its ring read as a device would), the message decoding (`controller::`), the mixer (`mixer::`) and the effects (`colorfx::`, `filters::`, `fxkit::`, `simd::`).
