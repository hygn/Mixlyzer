# AGENTS: analyzer/ (Mixlyzer)

The details of `analyzer/`: its boundary and every analysis. The root `AGENTS.md`'s rules (run, layers, owners, change guidelines) apply here too; read it first.

## Map
- `analyzer/`: the analyzer, complete in its directory: it imports no app code (`tests/test_analyzer_boundary.py`; its own OS facts in `runtime.py`, its own FFmpeg lookup: one beside the folder holding `analyzer/`, else on PATH). The app hands it a file path and `settings.AnalysisSettings` (built from config.json by `core/workers/analysis_settings.py`: every model the file to read, the user's retrained file or the shipped one resolved there; the analyzer has no default model path); it hands back typed results (`pipeline.track.TrackAnalysis`) and reports stages (`pipeline/stages.py`) and refusals (`errors.AnalysisError` codes) by id, which the app words (`core/workers/analysis_texts.py`: no `tr()` in the analyzer). Where the results are stored and in what form (the NPZ blocks, the index rows) is the app's (`core/workers/analysis.py` `analyze_for_library`).
  - `pipeline/`: what is run. `track.py` the full analysis, every stage in one function (`analyze_track`); each of the editor's partial reanalyses its own file (`beatgrid.py` a tempo segment, `keys.py` the whole track or a selection, `phrases.py`, `jumpcues.py`: JumpCUE pairs, the app makes the block).
  - `audio/`: FFmpeg's decoder (also the player's and the tag reader's: every input through `decoder._input`, `file:` and the absolute path with `-protocol_whitelist file`, so a file's name or a playlist inside it opens no other protocol), the analyses' decode (`load.py` `fast_load`), HPSS, resampling, spectra, the overview waveform's measurement (`wave_overview.py`; drawn by `views/render/wave.py`).
  - `music/`: tempo rows (steady and ramps; the editor's grid moves: `canonical_inizio`, `shift_grid_in_seg`), key rows (`key_rows.py`), the beats and downbeats they make (`beat_geometry.py`).
  - `beat/`: BPM / beatgrid analysis.
    - `general/`: the full analysis's beat tracker (NumPy only; shipped weights pinned as `assets/weights/default_beat_tcn.npz` (the three trainings in one file), `default_beat_critic.npz`, `default_downbeat_ctx_hgb.npz`, `default_beat_decoder.json`, `default_refine_onset_hgb.npz`; the settings name the user's files (same names without `default_`), and a missing user file means the shipped one (resolved by the app: `system/model_files.py`); Reoptimize never writes a `default_` file): `tcn.py` frame TCN, `decode.py` section decoding, `gradual.py` ramps and drifting sections, `critic.py` metronome critic, `junctions.py` junction rules, `bars.py` bar check / bars / production rows, `tracker.py` the pipeline and the consensus of the three trainings.
    - The editor's segment reanalysis (`analyzer/pipeline/beatgrid.py`) uses the same models (the three TCN trainings averaged) on the segment plus 4 s of context: the steady grid with the most evidence (with a reference BPM: within 20 % of it), or a linear ramp when it explains the onsets better by one beat of evidence and moves a beat audibly (`gradual.ramp_or_steady`; guesses from the neighbours' tempos and the segment's halves), then static bars for its downbeat. Beside `general/`, the beat package holds what the tracker reads: `frame_features.py` (the frame features, also used by JumpCUE), `learned_onset.py` (the tempo-change onset, a GBM), `grid_refine.py` (`refine_boundaries` on that onset, `hold_slow_tail`), `downbeat_features.py` (the per-beat features of the contextual downbeat model) with `periodicity.py` and `melody_contour.py`; `analyzer/training/onset_training.py` is the onset models' training data (library tracks, onset channel cache, samples). Settings > Analysis > Beat: BPM min (the octave), Dynamic Analysis (off: one steady grid for the whole track) and Dynamic Downbeat (static analysis only: odd bars / meter changes, off = one bar length and phase; always on with dynamic analysis), and in Advanced the model paths (tempo-change onset, beat TCN, metronome critic, downbeat model, decoder costs `assets/weights/beat_decoder.json`: a new section / a same-tempo half-beat reset / an odd bar, in evidence units of the activation) with their feature caches; Reoptimize Beat Models retrains any of them on the library's grids (`analyzer/training/`): the decoder costs (`beat_decoder_optimizer.py`: every song tracked with each candidate combination, the costs with the least wrong time -- unusable beats + missed bars -- kept; refit them after the beat model changes, about an hour), the downbeat GBM (sklearn, minutes), the TCN and the critic fine-tuned from the current model in NumPy (`beat_tcn_optimizer.py`, `beat_critic_optimizer.py`: their own forward / backward passes, trainings in parallel processes on a memory-mapped copy of the frames; tens of minutes), all on the tracker's own frames cached by `analyzer/training/beat_frames.py` (22050 Hz, HPSS, hop 256, every FrameFeatures field in float16). The networks start from the current model, the shipped one, or from scratch, and the decoder costs are refitted around the current or the shipped costs (Quick: the half-beat reset and odd-bar costs only); `analyzer/training/model_start.py` holds the minimum song counts (blocked below 200 songs from scratch, 20 for fine-tuning, 50 for the GBMs) and the warnings the dialog shows. With CV a group of songs is held out first and the current and new models are compared on it. There is no beatgrid offset: grids are stored as the tracker gives them.
  - `key/`: key analysis. `chord_key.py` is the full analysis's key analyzer (NumPy only; weights `assets/weights/key_chord_cnn.npz`, a missing file = the shipped `default_key_chord_cnn.npz`): a root-equivariant chord CNN reads every beat (bass / treble CQT chroma and STFT chroma of the harmonic part, 22050 Hz) as P(chord | beat) over 24 triads, the key evidence is log sum_c P(c | beat) P(c | key, phrase position), and a bar Viterbi over the 24 keys sets the keys (a change costs J at a phrase start, J + JX elsewhere, half of it between relative keys, plus an interval prior from the annotated key changes: a minor third / half step / whole step is cheap, a fifth dear; excursions shorter than MINEX bars are removed). Keys are DJ / DAW keys: the scale (key signature, Alphanumeric number), major / minor by the tonic chord. The chord layer was trained through the key loss only (no chord labels). The editor's key reanalysis (`analyzer/pipeline/keys.py`) uses the same model: the whole track (the decoder) or a selected span (the key with the most evidence over its beats, read on the whole track). Settings > Analysis > Key: Dynamic Analysis (off: one key covering the whole track -- the dynamic analysis is run as it is and the key holding the most time in its INTRO / OUTRO / INTERLUDE phrases is taken, else the key holding the most time, the first of a tie; for the analysis and the editor's whole-track reanalysis), the model path, its feature cache and Reoptimize Key Model (`analyzer/training/key_model_optimizer.py`: the network fine-tuned from the current / shipped model or trained from scratch in NumPy, its own forward / backward, on the frame chroma cached per track; training songs are the tracks whose keys differ from the kept automatic result, and tracks analyzed before it was kept whose annotation does not have the old analyzer's raw shape; with CV a group of songs is held out and the whole key analysis, current vs new, is compared on it before saving; the decoder settings are kept). There is no other key analyzer. The track's key (library.db's `key`, the Export tab's tag) is the one the static analysis takes, whatever the setting: `analyzer.music.key_rows.main_key` of its key rows and phrases, the one rule (the static analysis, and library.db's rows of every writer, `core.library.segments.track_index`, read it).
  - `phrase/`: phrases and their labels, the LEAD_IN, the CUE points' rules (`cue_points.py`: by label, and AutoCUE's; the Phrase page's CUE button uses the same `phrase_cue_points`).
  - `jumpcue/`: JumpCUE detection (self-similarity, the jump point model), AutoCUE's JumpCUE (`autocue.py`).
  - `search/music/`: Search by Music, the library found by a hummed / sung / whistled melody or by the song itself played from a phone or a speaker, through the microphone (or a file); no mode is picked (`search.search_audio`): a voice's pitch by YIN for all frames at once first (`query.py`, ~0.1 s; a whistle told by its nearly pure tone above 950 Hz and tracked in its own range; a single voice or instrument: `VOICE_LIKE` of its frames with a clear period); then spectral-peak landmarks, tens of ms, for a recording of the song itself (`landmarks.py`, Wang 2003: peak pairs hashed, the song whose hashes line up at one offset, voted in NumPy on the index's rows of the query's hashes; a mix's query keeps three times the index's peaks, `QUERY_PEAKS_PER_SEC` / `QUERY_FAN_OUT`, so a crowd's calls and screams, which take a second's strongest peaks, leave the song's, at least `MIN_VOTES`; a single voice is searched at the index's density, at least `VOICE_MIN_VOTES`; then nothing else is searched); else at once on three threads: for a mix, the landmarks through a DJ's tempo with the key locked (`landmarks.search_tempo`: the query's peak times scaled to the song's by each of `TEMPO_FACTORS`, +-8 % in 1 % steps, paired again and voted at once; at least `MIN_TEMPO_VOTES`; measured on a DJ set's room microphone against its master: the right song first 0.44 -> 0.72 joined over time, ~300 ms a search), the voice's pitch (every `QUERY_HOP`, 23 ms), and, for a mix, the melody extractor's line of the query (read as the references are) within +-1 semitone (a recording played at another tempo or pitch; kept only under `MUSIC_MAX_COST`, a quarter semitone a frame: the extractor finds lines in noise too), the two melody searches' songs joined by their lower cost; the voice and the extractor's line against two reference lines a song (`melody.py`: the predominant melody of the stereo centre, where a lead vocal is mixed (the mix's spectrum weighted by how alike left and right are, its harmonic part by the mix's HPSS mask in the same STFT: `harmonic_parts`, no HPSS of its own), and the mix's top line, the highest of its 3 strongest contours: an instrumental intro's riff; both from `analyzer.beat.melody_contour` (its CQT salience: a salience from the STFT itself, as MELODIA's, took half the time but found 3 - 4 points fewer songs over 416 queries); in the analysis the mix's contours are the ones its frame features found in the same harmonic part, handed over by `melody_contour.take_contours`, so they are not found twice; at 0.4 / 0.2 / 0.1 s frames, u8 in thirds of a semitone) by a subsequence DTW cascade over all songs (`matcher.py`: tempo x0.5-2, the octave folded, a transposition lane per third of a semitone within +-3 semitones, all lanes as SIMD, the kernels without the GIL on `SEARCH_THREADS` threads (a quarter of the cores, 4 at most: never all of them beside the GUI); `SEARCH_STAGES`: whole semitones over all songs at 0.4 s, every third at 0.2 s, the thirds around the key at 0.1 s; about 0.5 s at 10,000 songs); the microphone's window under -55 dBFS is not searched. Each song's entry (`index.py`) is made by the full analysis, for tracks analyzed before by Index Now (`pipeline/music_index.py`); the searches of the last seconds are joined over time while listening (`listening.py`); the app keeps the index, `{libpath}/music_search.db` (`core.library.music_index`: `core/library/AGENTS.md`), and lends its landmarks to the search (`landmarks.LandmarkIndex`, a Protocol). The research behind it (synthetic queries, what failed) is in `research/benchmark/qbh_research` (not committed).
  - `search/variants.py`: the library's Variants, its tracks that are versions of one song (the same audio: another encode, copy or rate; another vocal; the instrumental / off vocal; an edit, a TV size; not arrangements, remixes or bootlegs: another song's form), found from the analysis's features alone, no audio (frames on the beat grid, subdivided so the frame rate sits in 200 - 400 a minute (a grid read at half or double tempo gives the same frames; a query near the octave's edge also tries the neighbouring one), per frame Philips-style bits of the overview's 12 bands, 11 over the mix + 5 over the backing bands (20 - 200 Hz, 6.3 - 20 kHz), their reliability, and the vocal range's level; index rows the backing bits of 6 frames at every 8th frame, a query every frame with its 6 weakest bits flipped, votes per (track, offset), the candidates verified on the stored codes: 32-frame stretches whose reliable backing bits agree >= 0.92, related when the mix's bits agree >= 0.8 on them and the shorter track is half covered; the same mix at >= 0.98, an instrumental by its vocal range 2 dB lower on a quarter of the frames, an edit by the covers differing by 0.2; ~20 ms a track with its query; measured on 1353 tracks queried against each other: 450 relations, no different song called a version; `track_fingerprints`, a stage of the full analysis, for tracks analyzed before made from their NPZ); the relations as the app uses them are `core/search/variants.py` (the groups), its index `core.library.variants` (`core/library/AGENTS.md`). The research behind it is `research/benchmark/fingerprint_research` (not committed).
  - `search/mixability.py`: the Mix Candidates' bars, where the end of one track (A) blends into the start of another (B). Per beat its features (`BeatFeatures`: the frame features' mix CQT chroma, the mean over the beat; the percussive part's three bands' flux, the maximum in each twelfth of the beat; the full analysis pools the frame features it has, ~20 ms; `beat_features_of_audio` reads them for a track analyzed before (HPSS, the CQT, the percussive STFT: ~6 s a track, `pipeline/mix_features.py`), the same as the analysis's: `beat.frame_features.chroma_and_percussive_bands` shares the frame features' own functions), per bar (downbeat to downbeat) a row: energy (the level and the 12 overview bands' balance), rhythm (the onsets folded over the bar's beats, normalized), harmony (the bar's chroma in the Tonal Interval Space); a bar's distance the components' weighted mean, each in its unit (`SCALE`: the median between random bars of a 420-track library). `best_overlap`: A leaves where a whole INTERLUDE / OUTRO run after its first chorus ends, B comes in from its top (a later phrase head before its first chorus only when none fits, each bar later costing), the overlap from a phrase head of both to a phrase boundary of both, as many bars each (2 - 16), on a steady grid (one beat count, each bar within 3 % of the median), the most alike (mean bar distance, a longer one a little better). Checked on the user's playlists (121 tracks played in a row against random pairs): each component nearer on the tracks played in a row (AUC 0.59 - 0.61), their sum 0.63; a both-singing penalty (the overview's vocal range) went the other way and is left out. The candidates themselves (the criteria, the rank) are `core/search/mixability.py`'s, the index `core.library.mixability`. The research behind it (barmix, rendered blends listened to) is `research/benchmark/fingerprint_research` (not committed).
  - `training/`: Reoptimize's optimizers (each retrains a learned model or refits settings on the library's annotations; their process and progress UI are the app's, `core/workers/optimizer.py`, the dialogs' warnings `core/workers/training_models.py`). A run is given the library's tracks with what it reads of their stored analyses (`library_tracks`, each with its `Annotation`, made by `core.analysis_library.jobs.training_annotations`: the arrays as stored, the JumpCUEs as each group's cue points) and the files it reads and writes; it opens no library file. What it reports and refuses is marked with `text.msg` (English source, values) and worded by the app (`training_models.words`, the catalogs' msgids; `build/translations.py` extracts `msg`), a refusal a `text.TrainingError`. Its process pools are `analyzer.runtime.spawn_pool`.

The full analysis's order as the app runs it, from the request to what the library stores, is `core/AGENTS.md`'s Analysis Flow; the editor's partial reanalyses its Segment Reanalysis.
