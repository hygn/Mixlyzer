# AGENTS: ui/ and views/ (Mixlyzer)

The details of the interface: `ui/` (its widgets, dialogs and screens) and `views/` (the plots). The root `AGENTS.md`'s rules (run, layers, owners, change guidelines) apply here too; read it first.

## Map

## UI Notes
- `ui.pane.MainPane` owns the top-level plot stack, overview, track info, transport controls, editor panel, and library widget. Above the record MT and RST. The transport column is stop, a yellow CUE (an icon, `assets/icons/transport_cue.png` / `_lit`; pressed and let go both sent; lit / blinking as the deck reports its lamp) and play; the tempo fader's steps follow the reported range (6: 0.02 %, 10 / 16: 0.05 %, WIDE: 1 %), the range button (its text the range, WIDE included) is the column's head in place of a Tempo label. The faders send requests and show the reported tempo / volume (not while the hand is on them). The peak meter (`track_info_panel.VerticalPeakMeter`) is a DJM-A9's channel level indicator: 15 segments, -30 to +12 dB in 3 dB steps, 0 dB at -12 dBFS (one of the A9's reference levels), so +12, red, is 0 dBFS, where the output clips; the engines' peak floor (`PEAK_FLOOR_DBFS`, -45) lies below its lowest segment; it rises at once and falls 72 dB/s (a segment in about 40 ms), its peak held 0.5 s, then falling 108 dB/s. Right of the peak meter the player's channel knobs (`TrackInfoPanel.knobs`, `ui/widgets/knob.py`), top to bottom as a DJ mixer's: TRIM, HI, MID, LOW; turned they send `sig_knob_requested` (TRIM: the trim on either engine, `audioengine.volume.TRIM_DB_RANGE` -24 - +9 dB; the EQ: the native engine's mixer, disabled on the Python engine) and they show the reported knobs (`DeckReport.knobs`; the Python deck reports its trim). The trim the deck reports (its knob, the settings, a controller) is kept in deck.json (`AppWindow._keep_trim`: the peak meter's gain at once, the file a second after it last moved), as the tempo range is. The editor sends its beats, downbeats, CUE points and JumpCUEs to the deck when one of them was replaced (`EditPanel._send_deck_marks`, checked every 100 ms).

- The main view's detail waveform (`views/waveform.py`) is rendered from the playback PCM in 1 s chunks on its render thread (`_WaveformRenderWorker`, `RENDER_BATCH_SIZE` at once, one started every `RENDER_SPAWN_INTERVAL_MS`): the pending chunk nearest the playhead starts first (ahead before behind at one distance); a scrub let go (the overview's or the main plot's drag) or a jump of the playhead (past the next chunk, not while scrubbing: a seek, a CALL, a JumpCUE, a beat jump; seen by the view in the reported playhead itself; jumping frame after frame, a controller's jog search, it is followed and resumed once where it stops) drops the requests made on the way and starts the chunk playback goes on from at once (on a thread of its own when the slots are busy). A layer's chunks farther from the playhead than `RENDER_KEEP_VIEWS` (2.5) of the widest view it draws are dropped (the long views' 60 s, the short ones' 22.5 s, the close ones' 7.5 s: their fine frame's chunks four times the long one's), except as many again shown most recently; a layer no view width draws any more keeps only what is near. A canvas made anew (other audio: another track, the track decoded again) counts a generation, which every request and image carries: one rendered for the audio before, still running then, is neither shown nor taken as rendered (two tracks not analyzed yet share a style, so it would land in the new track's layer). The thread is stopped at `detach` and when the app quits (`aboutToQuit`): the views are freed after `app.exec()` returns, and a QThread destroyed running aborts the process.

## Rendered just in time
- The key strip is rendered just in time from `key_segments` (`views.render.keystrip.render_keystrip`); no image is stored.
- The track as heard: `MainPane.send_played` sends `EventBus.sig_played_changed(speed, semitones)` (the tempo the deck reports; the key shift, plus the tempo's semitones with MT off, rounded together) at each change and again when the views are made anew or Settings > View changes. With Settings > View > Display's "Show the tempo and key as played" (`viewconfig.played_tempo_key`, off by default) the overview's and the main view's BPM labels are times the speed and their keys (strip colours, labels) moved by the semitones (`views.base.shown_played`, `views.render.keystrip.transposed` / `scaled_tempo_segments`; the times as they are); off, the track's own. The editor's Key page lights its key changes (semitone, fifth, relative: the warning colour, icons inverted; the key picker framed in it) while the key heard is not the track's, as TAP is while the tempo is changed: what they change is still the track's own keys.
- The overview waveform and the main view's preview (its stand-in until the detail chunks are drawn) are rendered just in time from the stored overview (`views.render.wave.render_wave_overview` / `render_wave_detail_preview`) in the style of `viewconfig.waveform_style` (RGB, or rekordbox-style 3Band; the overview's bar heights are the bands' power in both, stacked with the 3Band weights: RGB one bar coloured by the band mix against the track's usual balance (`wave.overview_colors`: each column's colour first: each band over its median where the track is audible, so the phrases come out in different colours; cubed, so the band clearly above its usual level leads the colour; red / green / blue for low / mid / high over the strongest as Mixxx's and rekordbox's RGB waveforms; in OKLab one lightness, moved by the brightness: the spectral centroid of the 12 bands against the track's usual one, darker towards the low band's red, brighter towards the high band's blue (a filter sweep inside one range shows); then smoothed in hue over 0.5 s, so it does not flicker: the mean colour's direction and the mean chroma apart, so hues taking turns within a beat never average to grey; the chroma at least the most every hue holds at that lightness; silence neutral), 3Band the bands stacked in their colours; the preview keeps the min / max): low / mid / high are summed from the 12 stored bands with the ranges of Settings > View > Waveform bands (`viewconfig.env_*`, weighted by log-frequency overlap), so a change of the ranges redraws them at once without re-analysis; no image is stored. The detail waveform is rendered from the playback PCM (its three-band scale is the stored `wave_detail_band_ref`).- JumpCUEs are kept in two states in the NPZ: `jump_cues_np` (the user's, replaced by edits) and `jump_cues_auto_np` (the automatic result, replaced only by full analysis or JumpCUE reanalysis). The JumpCUE optimizer (settings → Analysis → CUE) learns from where they differ; do not write edits into `jump_cues_auto_np`.
